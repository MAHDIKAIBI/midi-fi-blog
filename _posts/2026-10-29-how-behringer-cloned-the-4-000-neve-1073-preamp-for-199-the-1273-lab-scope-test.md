Originally designed in 1970 for the Wessex A88 console, the 1073 is an all-discrete, single-ended, unipolar Class-A preamplifier operating from a single positive 24-volt direct-current power rail.</p><p>Audio signals enter through a shielded Marinair 10468 input transformer, offering selectable 300-ohm or 1,200-ohm impedance matching. The mic-level voltage cascades through two discrete transistor gain stages built on modular BA284 and BA283 daughterboards. The signal then feeds a passive three-band inductor-capacitor (LC) equalization network featuring a 12 kHz fixed high shelf, an inductor-based semi-parametric mid band, and a Baxandall low shelf with stepped turnover frequencies. Finally, a heavy-duty 2N3055 power transistor drives a gapped Marinair LO1166 output transformer, keeping the core isolated from DC saturation.</p><p>For boutique manufacturers assembling these units by hand, the hardware costs escalate rapidly:</p><ul><li><strong>Input Transformer:</strong> Sourcing an authentic UK-made Carnhill or Marinair-spec input transformer costs upwards of $85.</li><li><strong>Gapped Output Transformer:</strong> Sourcing a heavy gapped output transformer adds another $90.</li><li><strong>Switchgear:</strong> Gold-contact Swiss Elma stepped rotary switches cost roughly $60 each, with multiple switches required per channel.</li><li><strong>Ferrite Inductor Array:</strong> Custom-tapped ferrite pot-core inductors add another $60.</li><li><strong>Assembly Labor:</strong> Between three to six hours of point-to-point through-hole hand-wiring costs over $400 in skilled labor.</li></ul><p>By the time a boutique channel strip leaves the factory gate, raw component and manufacturing costs easily surpass $800. Hand-wired turret boards allow any resistor, capacitor, or transistor to be desoldered and replaced in minutes with basic workbench tools.</p><p>The Behringer 1273 relies instead on commercial-grade detented potentiometers and sealed miniature relays rated for 5,000 to 10,000 cycles.
